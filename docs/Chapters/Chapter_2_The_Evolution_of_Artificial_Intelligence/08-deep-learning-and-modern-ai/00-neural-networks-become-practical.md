## What changed?

Neural networks were not new, but training larger ones had been difficult. During the 2000s, three conditions began to improve together:

- **More data:** Larger collections of examples gave networks more to learn from and more cases on which to test their predictions.
- **Faster hardware:** Graphics processing units (**GPUs**) could perform many calculations in parallel, making larger training runs more practical.
- **Improved training:** Better network designs and training techniques helped those larger runs learn useful patterns.

## Why did this matter?

Together, these changes made it increasingly practical to train networks with more layers and more adjustable connections. They set the stage for the image-recognition results that followed in the early 2010s.

## Key insight

**The ideas alone were not enough.** Data, computing power, and ways to train the networks had to come together.
