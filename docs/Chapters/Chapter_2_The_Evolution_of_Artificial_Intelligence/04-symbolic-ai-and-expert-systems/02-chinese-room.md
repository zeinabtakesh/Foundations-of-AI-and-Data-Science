# The Chinese Room

<div class="chapter-intro" markdown>

**1980 · Does following rules mean understanding?**

The Chinese Room is a thought experiment by philosopher [John Searle](#john-searle){ data-open-details }. It asks whether producing the right answers is enough to say that a system understands what those answers mean.

[← Timeline](../index.md){ .chapter-back title="Back to chapter timeline" }

</div>

## What is the thought experiment?

Imagine a person locked in a room who **does not understand Chinese**. Someone outside passes in a question written in Chinese. Inside the room, the person follows detailed instructions for matching symbols and sends back a convincing answer in Chinese.

## Key insight

Searle argued that **following rules for symbols is not, by itself, understanding their meaning**. The room might produce correct answers while its operator understands no Chinese. Critics reply that perhaps the **whole system**, not the person alone, could understand. The argument remains debated; it is not a test that settles what every AI system understands.
