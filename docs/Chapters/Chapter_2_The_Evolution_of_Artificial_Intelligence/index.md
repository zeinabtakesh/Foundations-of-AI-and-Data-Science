# Chapter 2 — The Evolution of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence did not appear as one single invention.

It developed through a sequence of changing ideas about **computation, reasoning, learning, and intelligence**.

A useful way to follow the chapter is through the questions researchers were trying to answer:

```text
What is computation?
        ↓
Can machines reason?
        ↓
Can machines learn?
        ↓
Why did early approaches fail to scale?
        ↓
Can machines learn complex representations from data?
        ↓
How did this lead to modern AI?
```

Two broad approaches appear repeatedly throughout the story:

- **Symbolic AI** — intelligence represented through explicit symbols, rules, and logical reasoning.
- **Connectionist AI** — intelligence emerging from networks of simple computational units whose connections can be learned.

The history is not a straight line. It moves through periods of optimism, technical limitations, AI winters, revived ideas, and major breakthroughs.

This page is the **map of the chapter**. Each milestone can be opened as a deeper page.

# Timeline

## 1. Foundations of Computation and Machine Intelligence

### 1936 — [Turing Machine](01-foundations/01-turing-machine.md)

**Alan Turing**

A mathematical model that formalized the idea of computation.

↓

### 1943 — [McCulloch–Pitts Neuron](01-foundations/02-mcculloch-pitts-neuron.md)

**Warren McCulloch & Walter Pitts**

One of the earliest mathematical models connecting neurons, logic, and computation.

↓

### 1950 — [Turing Test](01-foundations/03-turing-test.md)

**Alan Turing**

A behavioral way of asking whether a machine can appear intelligent.

---

## 2. AI Becomes a Field

### 1956 — [Dartmouth Workshop](02-ai-becomes-a-field/01-dartmouth-workshop.md)

**John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Nathaniel Rochester & Claude Shannon**

Artificial intelligence emerges as a distinct research field.

↓

### 1950s — [Early Symbolic Reasoning](02-ai-becomes-a-field/02-early-symbolic-reasoning.md)

**Logic Theorist and related systems**

Computers begin manipulating symbols and searching through reasoning steps.

↓

### 1957–1958 — [Perceptron](02-ai-becomes-a-field/03-perceptron.md)

**Frank Rosenblatt**

A neuron-like model whose weights can be learned from examples.

↓

### 1959 — [Samuel's Checkers Program](02-ai-becomes-a-field/04-samuel-checkers.md)

**Arthur Samuel**

An early demonstration of a computer improving performance through experience.

↓

### 1966 — [ELIZA](02-ai-becomes-a-field/05-eliza.md)

**Joseph Weizenbaum**

Pattern matching and hand-written rules produce surprisingly convincing conversation.

↓

### 1969 — [Limits of the Single-Layer Perceptron](02-ai-becomes-a-field/06-perceptron-limitations.md)

**Marvin Minsky & Seymour Papert**

The limitations of simple perceptrons become widely recognized.

---

## 3. First AI Winter

### 1970s — First AI Winter

Early expectations collide with limited compute, limited models, combinatorial explosion, and difficult real-world problems.

---

## 4. Symbolic AI and Expert Systems

### 1970s–1980s — Expert Systems

AI increasingly focuses on encoding specialized human knowledge through facts and rules.

↓

### 1980 — Chinese Room

**John Searle**

A philosophical challenge about whether correct symbol manipulation is the same as understanding.

---

## 5. Neural Networks Return

### 1982 — Hopfield Networks

**John Hopfield**

Recurrent neural networks demonstrate associative-memory behavior.

↓

### 1980s — Multi-Layer Networks and Backpropagation

Practical methods for training multi-layer neural networks become increasingly important.

---

## 6. Second AI Winter

### Late 1980s–Early 1990s — Second AI Winter

Expert systems, specialized AI hardware, and other approaches face cost, scalability, and expectation problems.

---

## 7. Statistical Machine Learning

### 1990s–2000s — Learning from Data

AI increasingly shifts from manually written rules toward statistical methods that learn patterns from data.

---

## 8. Deep Learning and Modern AI

### 2000s–2010s — Neural-Network Revival

More data, more computing power, and better training methods make larger neural networks practical.

↓

### 2012 — AlexNet

**Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever & Geoffrey Hinton**

Deep neural networks, ImageNet, and GPUs produce a major computer-vision breakthrough.

↓

### 2016 — AlphaGo

Neural networks, search, and reinforcement learning achieve superhuman performance in Go.

↓

### 2017 onward — Transformers and Large-Scale AI

Transformers become a foundation for modern language models and many later AI systems.
