## What is the difference?

**Symbolic AI** represents knowledge with explicit symbols and rules. An [expert system](../04-symbolic-ai-and-expert-systems/01-expert-systems.md) might use a rule such as “if these symptoms are present, consider this diagnosis.” Its reasoning can often be inspected, but building and maintaining enough rules is hard.

**Connectionism** builds systems from connected units whose weights produce learned behavior. A [perceptron](../02-ai-becomes-a-field/03-perceptron.md) is a small example; later neural networks are much larger. Their patterns are learned from data, but the reason for a particular output may be harder to express as a short rule.

## How do they relate?

These are traditions, not sealed boxes. Some systems combine learned perception with explicit reasoning or constraints. A model might recognize objects in an image, while a rule-based component checks whether a proposed action is allowed. Such **hybrid approaches** try to use the strengths of both; they do not automatically solve either one's limitations.

## Key insight

The history of AI is not a simple handoff from rules to networks. Both ways of representing knowledge still inform the field.
