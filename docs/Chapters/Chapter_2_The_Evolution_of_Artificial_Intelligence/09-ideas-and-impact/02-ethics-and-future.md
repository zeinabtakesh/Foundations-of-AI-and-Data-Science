## What matters beyond performance?

An AI system can score well on a benchmark and still cause harm in use. **Bias** can make errors fall unevenly across groups. **Privacy** matters when training or operating a system involves personal data. **Transparency** asks whether people can understand a system's purpose, limits, and grounds for a decision. **Work** may change as tasks are automated or reorganized; effects depend on the job and how a tool is deployed.

These are not problems that one accuracy number can settle. They require choices about data, evaluation, oversight, and who bears the consequences.

## What might come next?

The book points to fairer AI, **edge AI** running closer to where data is produced, and AI in **robotics**. It also mentions **quantum computing** as a possible future direction, not as an established solution to today's AI limitations. Each direction brings its own technical and social questions.

## Key insight

AI's history includes changing capabilities *and* changing responsibilities. Asking who benefits, who is affected, and how a system can be checked belongs alongside asking how well it performs.
