# Early Symbolic Reasoning

<div class="chapter-intro" markdown>

**1956 · Reasoning with symbols**

Early AI researchers explored whether a computer could reason by following explicit rules.

[← Timeline](../index.md){ .chapter-back title="Back to chapter timeline" }

</div>

## What is it?

Symbolic reasoning represents knowledge as **facts and rules** written in a form a computer can manipulate. Early programs used these human-written rules to reach conclusions.

## How it works

A program applies a rule when its conditions match the facts it knows. For example: **every robot is a machine** + **Ada is a robot** → **Ada is a machine**. More rules can extend the chain.

## Key insights

The **Logic Theorist**, built by [Allen Newell](#allen-newell){ data-open-details }, [Herbert A. Simon](#herbert-simon){ data-open-details }, and [J. C. Shaw](#cliff-shaw){ data-open-details }, showed that a computer could search for mathematical proofs. Its steps could be inspected, but the approach depended on carefully supplied rules and knowledge.
